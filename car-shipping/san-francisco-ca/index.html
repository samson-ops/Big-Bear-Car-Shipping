San Francisco auto transport guide

Why shipping a car from San Francisco takes local know-how

A standard car carrier is a tractor pulling a two-level trailer, about 80 feet end to end and nearly 14 feet tall when loaded. It cannot climb Filbert Street, cannot turn at the top of Lombard, and will not fit under the Muni wires on some of the routes a sedan takes without thinking. Most San Francisco pickups therefore happen at a meeting point rather than at the curb outside your flat. Where that point is, and how the driver gets there, is the part Big Bear's San Francisco desk is built to handle.

How pickups work inside the city

The driver and your coordinator agree on a meeting point before the truck is dispatched. In practice the good spots are flat, wide and legal to stop in for twenty minutes: the Marina Green side of Marina Boulevard, the wide stretches of Sunset Boulevard, parts of Great Highway, the big lots around Stonestown and Mission Bay, and industrial blocks in Bayview and Dogpatch. The driver cannot double-park on Divisadero or block a Muni lane, and a tow in San Francisco is expensive for everyone, so your coordinator will not agree to a spot that doesn't work.

Three local rules to plan around:

  • Street cleaning and permit zones. Make sure the car isn't parked somewhere it will be ticketed or towed if the pickup shifts a day. Carriers run on windows, not appointments.
  • Hills. If your car lives on a block with a grade over about 15 percent, tell us. The driver needs the car on flat ground to load it, and some low cars can't make it onto the ramp from a slope.
  • Garages. Residential garages in SF are often tight and sloped. Have the car out on the street or at the meeting point before the truck arrives rather than asking the driver to extract it.

Where we serve around the Bay

The San Francisco desk covers the city, the Peninsula down through San Mateo County, Marin across the Golden Gate, and the inner East Bay. East Bay and Peninsula pickups are usually simpler and sometimes cheaper, because the streets are wider and carriers already stage along the 880 corridor in Oakland and San Leandro. If you have a choice of where to hand over the car, say at a relative's house in Alameda or at a friend's place in Daly City, mention it when you quote.

Routes and timing from San Francisco

Eastbound trucks cross the Bay Bridge and climb the Altamont to Interstate 5 and then Interstate 80 over Donner Pass, or take the 5 south and the 10 or 40 east. Donner closes for chains or snow several times each winter; your coordinator watches the forecast and may route a December shipment south to avoid it, which adds a day. Typical lanes:

What influences the price in San Francisco

The city's quirks show up in pricing in two small ways. Drivers budget extra time for a San Francisco pickup, and fewer carriers originate in the city than in the East Bay or Los Angeles. Both nudge rates a little higher than a comparable pickup in Oakland. The larger factors are the ones that apply everywhere: the size and weight of the vehicle, open versus enclosed, whether it runs, how firm your dates are and the season. Summer and late August, when the universities fill up and tech relocations peak, are the busiest months. Give the carrier a flexible window and the rate drops.

Enclosed transport and the salt-air question

San Francisco produces a lot of enclosed shipments relative to its size. Part of that is value: Pacific Heights and Presidio Heights garages hold a lot of cars that belong under a roof during a cross-country trip. Part of it is the climate. Owners of classics and collector cars kept near the ocean often ship enclosed simply to avoid adding road grime and salt spray on top of what the fog has already done. If your car is low, rare, freshly painted or simply something you would be upset to see with a rock chip, enclosed is worth the premium of roughly 40 to 60 percent. Everything else rides open, the same way new cars arrive at dealerships on Van Ness.

Who ships from the city

Relocations lead, in both directions: people leaving for Austin, Denver, New York and Seattle, and people arriving for jobs who want a car waiting for them. Many San Francisco residents live without a car and inherit or buy one elsewhere; we deliver into the city as readily as we ship out. Students at UCSF, USF and San Francisco State ship in August and out in May. Collectors ship to and from auctions and concours events. And every spring, a steady stream of snowbird cars comes back up from Palm Springs and Scottsdale.

Preparing the car

Wash it for an accurate inspection, keep fuel at a quarter tank, remove the FasTrak tag, parking placards and anything loose, and photograph all sides. Make sure the battery is healthy; a car that has sat on a hill in the Outer Richmond for three months may not start on pickup day, and a non-running car needs a different truck. Have a key ready, get the car to the meeting point a few minutes early, and your coordinator takes it from there with updates at every stage.

San Francisco FAQ

Questions about shipping a car from San Francisco

Will the carrier come to my street in San Francisco?
Where the street is flat and wide enough for an 80-foot hauler, yes. On hills and narrow blocks, which covers much of the city, your coordinator arranges a meeting point nearby, such as a wide boulevard or a large parking lot. This is agreed before the truck is dispatched, so there are no surprises on pickup day.
Is it cheaper to have my car picked up in Oakland or on the Peninsula instead?
Often slightly, yes. More carriers stage along the 880 corridor and drivers budget less time for suburban pickups. If you can hand the car over in the East Bay or on the Peninsula, mention it when you request a quote and we will price both options.
How long does it take to ship a car from San Francisco to New York?
Plan on seven to ten days in transit plus a pickup window of one to five days. In winter, routing around Donner Pass closures can add a day. Expedited service shortens the pickup window.
Should I ship my classic car enclosed from San Francisco?
If the car is valuable, low, freshly restored or simply something you would hate to see chipped, yes. Enclosed carriers use liftgates and soft tie-downs and keep the car out of weather and road debris. Expect about 40 to 60 percent more than open transport.
Do you deliver cars into San Francisco for people who just bought one out of state?
Yes. Inbound delivery works the same way as pickup: the driver meets you at your address if it is reachable, or at a flat, legal spot nearby. Many buyers of cars from out-of-state dealers and private sellers use this service.
