Santa Clarita Valley auto transport guide

Shipping a car from the Santa Clarita Valley

Every car carrier that leaves Los Angeles for Northern California, Oregon, Washington or the Central Valley rolls up Interstate 5 past Valencia on its way to the Grapevine, and many of them stop at the truck plazas in Castaic to rest before the climb. For a Santa Clarita customer, that means a northbound slot is rarely hard to find, and an eastbound one is only a short hop down the 14 or the 5 to join the Los Angeles freight stream. Big Bear's Santa Clarita desk coordinates pickups across the valley and over the hill in the Antelope Valley. Price your route with the quote tool above, or call (661) 344-5333.

Who ships from Santa Clarita

The valley is a family town, and that shapes its shipping. The largest group is families relocating out of state, with Texas, Idaho, Tennessee, Arizona and Nevada at the top of the list, who fly the household and ship a second car rather than caravan. Students at College of the Canyons and CalArts ship cars in and out with the school year. Retirees in Valencia and Stevenson Ranch send cars to second homes or to grandchildren. The film and television production companies that work out of the valley's studios and ranches move picture cars on a schedule. And a steady stream of pickups and lifted trucks leaves Canyon Country and Agua Dulce for new owners across the West.

Routes and transit times from Santa Clarita

Northbound runs are the valley's specialty: over the Grapevine to Bakersfield, Fresno, the Bay Area and the Pacific Northwest. Eastbound shipments drop to the 210 and the 15, or take the 14 through Palmdale to Barstow and the 40. Representative lanes:

Where we pick up in the SCV

Most of Santa Clarita is easy for a full-size car hauler. Valencia's paseos and master-planned streets, Stevenson Ranch, Saugus and the newer tracts off Plum Canyon and Copper Hill have wide roads and big retail lots at every corner. The exceptions are the canyons. Sand Canyon, the hillside streets above Canyon Country, Agua Dulce's ranch roads and parts of Castaic and Val Verde have grades, tight turns and low branches that a 14-foot trailer cannot clear. There, your driver will meet you at the bottom of the canyon on Soledad, Sierra Highway or at a shopping center, agreed in advance. HOA-governed communities such as Westridge, Tesoro and Bridgeport may restrict commercial vehicles on interior streets; your coordinator checks before dispatch.

The Grapevine, wildfire season and the calendar

  • Tejon Pass closures. The Grapevine closes a few times each winter for snow or ice, and northbound trucks hold in Castaic until Caltrans reopens it. A December or January pickup headed north may shift by a day; your coordinator watches the forecast.
  • Wildfire season. Fires along the 5 and the 14 corridors have closed both freeways in recent years, sometimes for most of a day. When that happens, dispatch reroutes through the San Fernando Valley and the 405 or waits for the all-clear. We keep you informed either way.
  • Heat. Summer afternoons in Canyon Country and the Antelope Valley routinely top 100°F. Morning pickups are easier on everyone, and enclosed transport is worth considering for a vehicle with a dark interior or fresh wrap headed across the desert.
  • Peak season. June through August is busiest as families relocate before the school year. Book a week or more ahead with a flexible window.

Antelope Valley pickups

The Santa Clarita desk also covers Palmdale, Lancaster and the communities along the 14. Edwards Air Force Base generates a steady flow of PCS moves; carriers cannot enter the base, so pickups are set at an off-base address or outside the gate, with a military rate on every quote. The Antelope Valley's wide streets and flat terrain make curbside pickup simple, though a car on an unpaved road in the desert east of Lancaster needs a meeting point on pavement.

What a Santa Clarita shipment costs

Rates from the SCV track Los Angeles closely because the valley shares its carrier pool, and northbound lanes can price slightly better than from LA since the driver skips the city. The factors that move your quote are the usual ones: vehicle size and weight (a lifted Tundra or a Suburban takes more than one slot), open versus enclosed, whether the car runs, and how firm your dates are. A flexible one-to-two-week window is the easiest way to lower the number. Quotes are all-in: door-to-door, carrier cargo insurance, no fuel surcharge later.

Trucks, toys and enclosed transport

The valley ships a lot of pickups, and a lifted truck needs a carrier with the right deck height and weight capacity; give accurate lift and tire details when you quote. Side-by-sides and UTVs can ride on a hauler if they can be driven aboard. Enclosed service suits the restored classics and hot rods that show at Old Town Newhall's events, show trucks with custom paint, and anything headed across the desert in summer that you would rather keep out of the sun and dust. Expect about 40 to 60 percent more for enclosed, with a liftgate and soft tie-downs.

Preparing for pickup

Wash the car for an accurate inspection, keep fuel to a quarter tank, remove the FasTrak tag and anything loose, and take photos of every side. If the car has sat through a hot summer, check the battery and tire pressure the day before; a car that won't start or roll needs a winch truck. Have a key ready, confirm the meeting point if you are in a canyon or a gated community, and let your coordinator know who will be there if it isn't you. Updates follow at pickup, in transit and on delivery day.

Santa Clarita FAQ

Santa Clarita car shipping FAQ

How much does it cost to ship a car from Santa Clarita to Texas or Idaho?
Santa Clarita to Dallas typically runs $900 to $1,250 on an open carrier for a standard sedan with four to five days in transit; Santa Clarita to Boise usually lands between $750 and $1,000 with about three days in transit. Enclosed, oversized and expedited shipments cost more.
Will a carrier come up Sand Canyon or into Agua Dulce?
Full-size haulers cannot manage the grades, tight turns and low branches on most canyon roads. Your coordinator arranges a meeting point at the bottom of the canyon, on Soledad or Sierra Highway, or at a nearby shopping center, before the truck is dispatched.
Can you ship a lifted truck from Canyon Country?
Yes. Give us the lift height, tire size and any aftermarket bumpers or racks when you quote so dispatch assigns a truck with the right deck height and weight capacity. Oversized trucks price above a standard pickup and may ship on a single-level carrier.
Does the Grapevine affect shipping from Santa Clarita?
Occasionally. Tejon Pass closes a few times each winter for snow or ice, and wildfire closures on the 5 or the 14 can hold trucks for part of a day. Either can shift a northbound pickup or delivery by a day; we monitor conditions and keep you informed.
Do you cover Palmdale, Lancaster and Edwards Air Force Base?
Yes. The Santa Clarita desk serves the Antelope Valley. Carriers cannot enter Edwards, so military pickups are arranged at an off-base address or outside the gate, with a military rate on every quote.
