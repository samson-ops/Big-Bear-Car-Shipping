Central Orange County auto transport guide

Shipping a car from Santa Ana and central Orange County

Santa Ana is the county seat and the geographic middle of Orange County, and the freeways reflect it: the 5 and the 55 cross at the city's edge, the 22 runs along its north side and the 57 is a few minutes away through Orange. Car carriers that serve the county stage in the industrial districts around the 55 and in nearby Anaheim, so a pickup here rarely waits long. Big Bear's Santa Ana desk puts that access to work: enter your ZIPs above for a live, all-in price, or call (714) 942-5576 and talk to a coordinator.

Pickups on Santa Ana's older streets

Much of Santa Ana was built before the master-planned cities to the south, and it shows in the streets. The historic neighborhoods north of downtown, such as Floral Park, French Park and Washington Square, have mature trees, narrow lanes and on-street parking on both sides, which is beautiful and a problem for a 14-foot-tall, 80-foot-long car hauler. The same goes for the dense blocks around Delhi and Artesia Pilar, where permit parking and tight corners are common. In those areas the driver will arrange to meet you on a wider street such as Main, Broadway, 17th or Bristol, or in a shopping center lot. Newer tracts in Tustin, Fountain Valley and the South Coast Metro area are usually straightforward curbside pickups.

A few local notes that keep pickup day simple:

  • Street sweeping. Check the posted days before leaving the car at a meeting point; carriers run on windows, not exact times.
  • Permit parking zones. If your block is permit-only, the truck can't idle there. Plan for a cross street.
  • Apartment complexes. Most won't admit a car hauler. Meet at the entrance or the nearest lot.

Family cars headed to Texas, Arizona and beyond

A large share of the Santa Ana desk's shipments are cars going to family: a parent sending a reliable sedan to a son starting work in Houston, a family moving to Phoenix who would rather fly the kids than caravan two cars, a grandparent's car going to a grandchild in Denver. These shipments are usually price-sensitive and date-flexible, which is a good combination; a two-week window on a busy lane like Santa Ana to Dallas or San Antonio lets dispatch find the cheapest truck with space. Tell your coordinator who will be releasing the car and who will be receiving it, and we handle the handoff paperwork at both ends.

Neighborhoods and nearby cities

The Santa Ana desk covers the city and central Orange County, from Fullerton and Anaheim in the north to Costa Mesa and Huntington Beach on the coast. Old Towne Orange, with its historic plaza and narrow streets, is another meeting-point area; the big lots around the Outlets at Orange and MainPlace are common choices.

Dealer and auction vehicles

The Santa Ana Auto Mall on Edinger and the dealer rows in Tustin and Anaheim generate steady outbound shipments to buyers in other states, and we move vehicles purchased at the auction yards in nearby Anaheim, Carson and the Inland Empire. For auction cars, have the lot number, buyer number and gate pass ready and tell us whether the car runs. Yards charge storage after a few days, so book as soon as the sale closes. For dealer-to-buyer shipments, we can release the vehicle to a named person and provide the bill of lading the buyer's lender may ask for.

What drives the price

Orange County shares its carrier pool with Los Angeles, so rates on the big lanes are among the lowest in the West. Within that, your quote moves on the size and weight of the vehicle, open versus enclosed, whether the car runs, and how firm your dates are. Summer and the weeks around the end of the school year are the busiest; January and February are the softest. Everything we quote is all-in: door-to-door service, carrier cargo insurance and no surcharge added after the fact.

Open or enclosed from Santa Ana

Open transport is the default and the right choice for a daily driver. Enclosed makes sense for the lowriders and restored classics that are a point of pride in the city, for show cars headed to events out of state, and for anything with a value or a paint job that you would rather not expose to a thousand miles of road debris. Enclosed carriers run liftgates and soft tie-downs and cost about 40 to 60 percent more than open.

Shipping into Santa Ana

Inbound shipments are as common as outbound ones. New hires arriving at the county's employers, students coming to Chapman and Cal State Fullerton, and buyers who found a car at an out-of-state dealer all need a vehicle delivered to central Orange County. Delivery works the same way as pickup: the driver meets you at your address if the street allows, or at a nearby flat, legal spot, and you inspect the car against the origin condition report before signing. If you can't be there, any adult you name can receive it.

Before the truck arrives

Wash the car so the condition report is accurate, keep fuel at a quarter tank, remove the FasTrak tag and personal items, and take photos of all four sides. If the car has sat for a while, check that it starts and that the tires hold air; a non-running car needs a winch truck. Have a key ready, confirm the meeting point, and let your coordinator know who will be there. Updates follow at pickup, in transit and on delivery day.

Santa Ana FAQ

Santa Ana car shipping FAQ

How much does it cost to ship a car from Santa Ana to Texas?
Santa Ana to San Antonio, Dallas or Houston usually runs $900 to $1,250 on an open carrier for a standard sedan, with four to five days in transit. Larger vehicles, enclosed transport and expedited pickup cost more.
Can the carrier pick up at my house in Floral Park or near downtown?
Often the driver will ask to meet on a wider street nearby, because the historic neighborhoods have narrow lanes, mature trees and parked cars on both sides. Main, Broadway, 17th and Bristol, or a shopping center lot, are common meeting points. Your coordinator confirms the spot before dispatch.
Can you ship a car I bought at an auction near Santa Ana?
Yes. We regularly move vehicles from the auction yards in Anaheim, Carson and the Inland Empire. Have the lot number, buyer number and gate pass ready and tell us whether the vehicle runs so the right truck is assigned.
Can someone else release the car for me?
Yes. Any adult you authorize can hand the car over and sign the bill of lading, and we can release the vehicle to a named person at the destination. Give your coordinator their name and phone number when you book.
How far ahead should I book from Santa Ana?
Five to ten days is ideal, with a flexible pickup window. Orange County has good carrier capacity, so common lanes can often be assigned within one to three days, but more notice means more trucks to choose from and a better rate.
